SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE CHARTER AND ENERGY TRANSITION DECARBONIZATION TARGETS
1.1 Decarbonization Roadmap to Net-Zero 2050
UST Global (UST), in alignment with Oman Vision 2040 and international greenhouse gas abatement commitments, establishes a binding corporate carbon reduction pathway. All asset operating centers (including Fahud, Marmul, Nimr, Lekhwair, Yibal, Qarn Alam, and Mina Al Fahal) are held to legally binding emission trajectories.
- By 2030: Mandatory 50% absolute reduction in Scope 1 and Scope 2 carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) emissions against the 2019 baseline.
- By 2040: 80% reduction in greenhouse gas operational intensity, transitioning baseline thermal power generation to green hydrogen and grid-scale solar photovoltaic arrays.
- By 2050: Certified Net-Zero carbon operations across all concession blocks.

1.2 Capital Allocation and Green Investments
A non-negotiable minimum of 24% of annual corporate capital expenditure (CAPEX) must be ring-fenced exclusively for low-carbon technologies, industrial electrification, high-efficiency turbomachinery replacements, and CCUS (Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage) pilot projects.

SECTION 2: METHANE ABATEMENT, LDAR PROTOCOLS, AND OPTICAL GAS IMAGING
2.1 Zero Routine Methane Emissions Mandate
Methane constitutes a potent greenhouse gas with a global warming potential 28 times greater than CO2 over a 100-year horizon. Under Ministerial Decree 104/2025, fugitive methane emissions from all compression, processing, and transportation facilities are subject to strict financial penalties:
- Standard penalty tariff: OMR 450 (USD 1,170) per metric ton of un-combusted methane released into the atmosphere.
- Continuous zero-tolerance threshold: Intentional venting of hydrocarbons during routine maintenance or pipeline pigging is strictly prohibited. Gas must be routed to flare, recycled through vapor recovery units (VRU), or re-injected.

2.2 Optical Gas Imaging (OGI) and Leak Detection and Repair (LDAR)
All field stations must execute mandatory monthly LDAR inspection sweeps using FLIR GF320 optical gas imaging cameras and drone-mounted tunable diode laser absorption spectroscopy (TDLAS):
- Any fugitive leak exceeding 500 ppm volumetric concentration must be tagged in SAP PM within 2 hours.
- Mechanical clamping or packing adjustment must be executed within 24 hours of discovery.
- Permanent certified engineering repair must be completed within 7 calendar days.

SECTION 3: ROUTINE FLARING ELIMINATION AND COMBUSTION EFFICIENCY
3.1 World Bank Zero Routine Flaring by 2030 Initiative
UST has been an official signatory to the World Bank Zero Routine Flaring initiative since 2017. All production and gas processing facilities must eliminate routine production flaring by 31-December-2026:
- Non-routine safety emergency flaring must achieve a minimum verified combustion efficiency of 98.5%.
- Flare tip steam or air-assist ratios must be continuously monitored via infrared pyrometry and optical flare meters (Fluenta FGM series).
- Monthly flaring volume allocations are capped at 0.42 million standard cubic meters per facility. Excursions above this limit incur a statutory surcharge of OMR 120,000 per occurrence.

SECTION 4: WATER STEWARDSHIP, PRODUCED WATER MANAGEMENT, AND NIMR REED BEDS
4.1 Produced Water Treatment and Deep-Well Disposal Minimization
Oil production operations across South Oman generate approximately 950,000 cubic meters of produced water daily. To eliminate deep-well disposal and protect local aquifers:
- Nimr Water Treatment Project (NWTP): Operates the world's largest commercial wetland produced water treatment facility, utilizing 1,350 hectares of engineered reed beds (Phragmites australis) and evaporation ponds.
- Reed-bed gravity treatment treats over 175,000 m3/day of produced water without electric power consumption, reducing corporate carbon emissions by 240,000 metric tons of CO2e annually compared to conventional deep-well disposal pumps.
- Heavy hydrocarbon concentration in effluent discharged to salt flats must not exceed 0.5 mg/L.

4.2 Groundwater Protection and Reverse Osmosis Recycling
At Fahud and Marmul central processing stations, treated produced water is desalinated through multi-stage industrial reverse osmosis (RO) plants to supply 100% of industrial wash water, turbine inlet air evaporative cooling water, and domestic field camp requirements. Zero fresh potable water from municipal aquifers may be used for industrial operations.

SECTION 5: CONTRACTOR HEALTH, SAFETY, AND ENVIRONMENT (HSE) AUDITS
5.1 Contractor Safety Management System (CSMS)
Over 70% of field operations are executed by third-party contractor personnel. All contractor and vendor partners must hold valid, active Level 1 CSMS pre-qualification certifications audited annually by UST's Directorate of HSE:
- Contractors must maintain a Total Recordable Injury Frequency (TRIF) of less than 0.22 per million man-hours.
- Any contractor experiencing a Lost Time Injury (LTI) is immediately subject to mandatory stop-work orders and formal joint investigation.
- Mandatory compliance with UST's Life-Saving Rules (12 non-negotiable safety rules governing work at height, energy isolation, confined space entry, and driving safety).

5.2 Vehicle Safety and In-Vehicle Monitoring System (IVMS)
Driving in desert operational concession blocks represents the single largest historical safety risk:
- All company and contractor vehicles must be equipped with certified satellite In-Vehicle Monitoring Systems (IVMS).
- Maximum speed limits are electronically capped: 100 km/h on paved concession highways, 70 km/h on graded desert tracks, and 30 km/h inside facility battery limits.
- Any recorded over-speeding event exceeding 5 minutes or driver fatigue alert results in automatic 30-day driver permit suspension.

SECTION 6: CORPORATE ETHICS, WHISTLEBLOWING, AND ANTI-BRIBERY
6.1 Zero-Tolerance Anti-Bribery and Corruption (ABC) Policy
UST operates under strict compliance with the Sultanate of Oman Anti-Corruption and State Audit Law (Royal Decree 111/2011) and international anti-bribery standards (ISO 37001):
- Employees, directors, and contractors are strictly prohibited from offering, soliciting, or accepting any gift, hospitality, commission, or financial inducement exceeding a nominal value of OMR 25.
- All high-value procurement tenders exceeding OMR 250,000 must undergo independent integrity due-diligence review by the Directorate of Internal Audit and State Financial and Administrative State Audit Institution (SAI).

6.2 Independent Whistleblower Hotline and Protection
An independent third-party whistleblower hotline (managed externally in Zurich, Switzerland) is available 24/7/365 to all employees, contractors, and local community members:
- Toll-free hotline: 800-7-9999 (Oman) / International: +41-44-800-9900
- Secure web portal: https://integrity.ust.com
- Absolute confidentiality and statutory non-retaliation protection are legally guaranteed for any bona fide report of safety breaches, financial irregularity, harassment, or environmental non-compliance.

SECTION 7: IN-COUNTRY VALUE (ICV) AND OMANIZATION EMPLOYMENT MANDATES
7.1 National Workforce Development and Omanization Quotas
In accordance with Ministerial Decision 22/2026, all operational concession holders must maintain progressive, verified Omanization employment quotas:
- Tier-1 Field Engineering and Maintenance Teams: Minimum 68.0% verified Omani technical personnel.
- Corporate, Finance, Digital, and Administrative Services: Minimum 85.0% verified Omani professionals.
- Executive Leadership and Station Superintendents: Minimum 90.0% verified Omani national leadership.
- Failure to achieve annual quota metrics results in statutory penalties of OMR 1,500 per unfulfilled quota role and forfeiture of state tax incentives.

7.2 In-Country Value (ICV) Local Sourcing
All engineering procurement must prioritize local suppliers and Omani manufacturers (Made in Oman):
- At least 42% of total supply chain procurement spend must be awarded to registered Omani Small and Medium Enterprises (Riyada).
- Mandatory inclusion of ICV scoring matrices (weighted at 30% of total commercial evaluation) in all major engineering contracts.
